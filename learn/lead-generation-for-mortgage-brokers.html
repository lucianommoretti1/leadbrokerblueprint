Lead generation for mortgage brokers and lenders

Mortgage lead generation in 2026 — rate-environment-sensitive, RESPA-regulated, with viable models for purchase, refinance, and HELOC.

The mortgage lead market in 2026

Mortgage lead generation is heavily rate-environment-dependent. In low-rate or refinance-cold environments, the market compresses 30-50%. In purchase-active environments (which 2026 leans toward), the market is robust. Operators who diversify across purchase, refinance, and HELOC weather the cycles better than single-product specialists.

Who buys mortgage leads

  • Independent mortgage brokers. Owner or lead loan officer decides. Volume: 5-50 leads/mo per buyer.
  • Mid-size mortgage banks (community lenders).
  • Retail divisions of large lenders: Rocket Mortgage, UWM, Pennymac retail.
  • Aggregators: LendingTree, Bankrate, Zillow Mortgage.

Pricing as of 2026

  • Shared form leads: $15-$50.
  • Exclusive form leads (refinance or purchase intent): $35-$120.
  • Phone-qualified leads with credit pull authorization: $80-$250.
  • Live transfer to LO on the line: $150-$500.

What makes a qualified mortgage lead

For purchase: timeline 0-6 months, pre-approval status known, credit range disclosed, property identified or actively searching. For refinance: current rate is 50+ basis points above current market, credit qualifying, equity position adequate.

Channels that work

Google Search (primary, especially for purchase), Meta paid (Lookalikes from past closed-loan customers), calculator/tool funnels (mortgage calculators are the canonical example of utility-driven lead gen), SMS/RCS opt-in funnels (mortgage shoppers respond well to text engagement), intent-data overlays (real-estate-listing-view signals), newsletter sponsorships in finance newsletters.

Compliance gotchas

  • RESPA: prohibits steering, kickbacks, undisclosed referral fees. Lead-buy relationships must be properly structured.
  • ECOA: equal credit opportunity. Don't ask demographic questions that could enable discriminatory practices.
  • TILA / Reg Z: APR disclosure required when advertising specific rates.
  • State licensing: most states require mortgage broker/lender licenses. Confirm buyer is licensed in consumer's state. Some states (NY, CA, NJ) have TPM (third-party marketing) disclosure requirements.
  • FCRA: any solicitation triggered by credit signals must comply with Section 604(c) prescreen rules.
