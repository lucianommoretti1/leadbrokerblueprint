Pay-per-call vs form leads: which model fits your operation?

Pay-per-call and form leads are the two dominant deliverable types in U.S. lead generation. They share customers and channels but they are mechanically different businesses with different unit economics, different operational stacks, and different compliance surfaces. Here's a practitioner-level comparison.

The deliverable

Form leads are submissions: name, contact info, qualification answers, consent record, delivered to the buyer's CRM via webhook or API. The buyer's intake team works the submission into a prospect.

Pay-per-call leads are inbound phone calls already routed to the buyer's line (or to a qualifying agent who warm-transfers). The buyer talks to the consumer in real time. Calls typically need to meet a minimum duration threshold (often 30, 60, or 90 seconds) to count.

Conversion rates

Pay-per-call calls convert dramatically better than form leads on a per-touchpoint basis — the consumer is on the phone, qualified, and engaged. Form-to-consult conversion typically runs 8-25% depending on vertical and speed-to-lead. Call-to-consult conversion typically runs 25-60%.

But form leads come in higher volumes at lower per-unit costs. The unit economics often work out similarly when you do the full math.

Operational stack

Form leads need: landing page builder, form, consent capture (TrustedForm/Jornaya), CRM, optional phone-qualification layer, delivery API to buyers. Lower operational complexity.

Pay-per-call needs: call platform (Ringba is the dominant platform), tracked numbers per campaign, IVR or live-agent qualification, whisper messages to buyers, recording with state-compliant disclosure, dispute resolution workflow. Higher operational complexity.

Compliance differences

Form leads: TCPA exposure on outbound follow-up calls/texts; consent capture is the critical control point; FTC Reviews Rule and CAN-SPAM matter for ongoing communication.

Pay-per-call: bigger TCPA surface because the deliverable IS the phone call; TSR (Telemarketing Sales Rule) applies more directly; recording-disclosure compliance is critical; some states (Florida, Oklahoma, Washington) have aggressive state-level rules that bite harder on call-driven operations.

Which fits which vertical

  • HVAC, plumbing, home services: both work; pay-per-call premium for emergency service categories.
  • Personal injury: pay-per-call dominates for high-value cases; form for volume sub-verticals.
  • Medicare: pay-per-call typical for AEP, form-driven for shoulder seasons.
  • Mortgage: form leads dominate; pay-per-call for high-intent refinance triggers.
  • Solar: hybrid; appointment-set deliverables most common.
  • Legal services (non-PI): form-heavy; some practice areas warm-transfer.

Which is better for new operators

Form leads for most new operators. Lower operational complexity, lower upfront stack cost, easier to recover from mistakes. Pay-per-call rewards experience — buyers expect quick-thinking agents on transfers, and the compliance bar is higher. Most successful pay-per-call operators came from form-lead operations first.
