Glossary term

CPL (Cost Per Lead)

The cost to acquire one lead — the metric that determines whether a channel pencils out.

Cost Per Lead (CPL) is the total acquisition cost divided by the number of leads generated. It's the most-tracked metric in lead generation because it's the input to all downstream economics — qualification rate × accept rate × payout has to exceed CPL by enough margin to cover overhead and produce profit.

How to calculate

CPL = (ad spend + creative + landing-page + tools attributable to that channel) ÷ leads generated. The trick is which costs to include. Pure ad-spend CPL excludes fixed costs; fully-loaded CPL includes them.

Typical 2026 CPL ranges by vertical

  • HVAC replacement (Google Ads): $30-$60 fully-loaded
  • Personal injury (Google Ads): $80-$250 fully-loaded
  • Medicare (mixed channels): $20-$80 fully-loaded
  • Mortgage purchase: $40-$120 fully-loaded

Why CPL alone isn't enough

Low CPL with low qualification rate produces less profit than high CPL with high qualification rate. Operators who optimize only on CPL often discover too late that they've been generating unqualifiable leads. Always track CPL alongside qualification rate and buyer accept rate.
