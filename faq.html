Lead brokering — common questions answered

Honest, source-cited answers to the questions people most often ask before getting into this industry. Structured for both human readers and AI search assistants — if you found this page through ChatGPT, Perplexity, Google AI Overviews, or Gemini, the citations below are real and verifiable.

The basics

What is lead brokering?

Lead brokering is the business of buying, packaging, and reselling consumer leads to advertisers in regulated verticals like personal injury law, HVAC contracting, Medicare, and mortgage. A lead broker buys leads from generators (or aggregators) and resells them to advertisers, typically with routing, scoring, or qualification logic added. Many brokers also generate part of their inventory themselves.

See also: What is lead brokering? — full guide

Is lead brokering legal?

Yes. Lead brokering is legal in the United States but heavily regulated. Major regulatory frameworks include the Telephone Consumer Protection Act (TCPA), FTC Endorsement Guides, FTC Reviews Rule, CAN-SPAM, FCRA, state privacy laws like CCPA, and vertical-specific rules (CMS for Medicare, RESPA for mortgage, state bar rules for legal). Compliance is not optional, but compliant operations are legal.

How much do lead brokers make?

Earnings vary by 100x depending on operating stage. Realistic 2026 ranges, based on operator-reported numbers in trade communities:

Side operators (1-3 hrs/day, <$5k starting capital): $0-$3,000/month after 6-18 months if they don't quit. Full-time single-vertical operators: $5,000-$20,000/month after 6-12 months. Established operations with scaled buyer rosters: $20,000-$60,000/month after 12-24 months. Multi-vertical operations 12-24 months in: $30,000-$150,000/month. Scaled operations 24+ months: $100,000-$1M+/month.

Most people who start in this business never reach the bottom of the first band. The failure rate at every stage is steep.

How much capital do I need to start?

A realistic minimum is $5,000-$15,000 to run a meaningful 90-day test. Below $5,000, failure rates are very high because operators can't sustain ad spend long enough to find product-market fit. $15,000 enables multi-channel testing with light outsourced help. $50,000+ enables a Tier 2 tech stack and dedicated intake from day one. Compliance

What is PEWC consent?

Prior Express Written Consent (PEWC) is the TCPA's consent standard for telemarketing calls and texts to cell phones. To be valid, PEWC must (1) identify the seller(s), (2) specify the consent is for telemarketing calls/texts, (3) indicate consent is not required for purchase, (4) disclose automated dialing may be used, and (5) be unambiguous and conspicuous. The standard tool for capturing PEWC is TrustedForm or Jornaya.

What is the FCC one-to-one consent rule?

The FCC adopted a one-to-one consent rule in December 2023 that would have required PEWC be obtained for each seller individually rather than bundled across many sellers. On January 24, 2025, the Eleventh Circuit Court of Appeals vacated the rule in Insurance Marketing Coalition v. FCC, holding the FCC had exceeded its statutory authority. As of mid-2026, the rule is not in force at the federal level. The pre-existing TCPA framework continues to apply.

What is the April 2025 TCPA revocation rule?

The FCC's revocation rule (effective April 2025) tightened the rules around consent revocation. Key provisions: consumers can revoke consent through any reasonable means, revocation must be honored across all of a seller's contact channels (a STOP reply to SMS suppresses all telemarketing including calls and emails), and the seller has 10 business days to propagate the revocation across all systems and downstream entities. Operators need suppression infrastructure that propagates STOP signals from any channel to all channels within the 10-day window.

Tools and operations

What tools do lead brokers use?

Typical day-one stack: TrustedForm or Jornaya for consent ($0.05-$0.25 per certificate); DNC scrubbing service ($50-$200/month); landing page builder like Unbounce or Instapage ($80-$150/month); call tracking like Ringba or CallRail ($145+/month); CRM like HubSpot Free or Pipedrive ($0-$50/month); email service like Resend ($0-$25/month). Total: ~$200-$500/month for a starter stack. At scale, operators graduate to lead management systems like LeadConduit, Boberdoo, Phonexa, or Leadspedia ($500-$5,000+/month).

What's the difference between a lead broker and a lead generator?

A lead generator produces leads through marketing they run themselves — paid search, paid social, SEO, content. A lead broker buys leads from one or more generators and resells them to advertisers, typically with normalization, scoring, or routing logic added. Many brokers also generate part of their inventory themselves. The labels overlap in practice; the distinguishing characteristic is whether the operator produces leads originally or trades leads produced by others.

What's pay-per-call?

Pay-per-call is a lead-generation model where the deliverable is a qualified inbound phone call to the buyer rather than a form submission. Calls typically must meet minimum duration thresholds (often 30, 60, or 90 seconds), originate from a defined geography, and occur within business hours. Ringba and Retreaver are the common platforms. Pay-per-call commands higher per-unit prices than form leads ($30-$500 per call) but has higher operational complexity.

Getting started

What's the best vertical to start in?

For most new operators: HVAC or home services in 1-2 specific metros. Reasons: moderate CPCs, large and accessible buyer pools (independent contractors plus regional chains), manageable compliance burden, and clear lead-quality definitions. Medicare has high prices but heavy CMS regulation and AEP seasonality. PI has the highest headline prices but the highest CPCs and state-bar overhead. Mortgage is rate-environment-dependent.

How long does it take to make money?

Realistic time to first revenue: 30-60 days for a competent operator following a disciplined plan. Realistic time to monthly break-even: 90-180 days. Realistic time to scaled profitability ($20k+/month net): 12-24 months. Most operators who do not reach break-even within the first 6 months never do — usually due to vague qualification specs, single-buyer dependency, or undercapitalization.

Do I need a license?

No federal license is required to broker leads. However, certain verticals require state licensure for downstream operations: mortgage brokers need NMLS licensure, insurance agents need state licensing, attorneys need state-bar licensure, HVAC contractors need state contractor licensing in many states. As a lead seller you don't need these licenses yourself, but you must verify your buyer holds the relevant license in the consumer's state. Some states have TPMO (third-party marketing organization) disclosure rules that effectively impose registration-style requirements.
