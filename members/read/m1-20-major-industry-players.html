Networks, exchanges, distribution platforms, and tooling vendors a new operator will hear named in trade conversations

How to use this reference

This is not a ranked list and is not an endorsement. It's a working reference covering the operators a newcomer will most often encounter in trade conversations, podcasts, and conference talks. Six categories are covered: lead distribution platforms, consent capture and verification, call routing and tracking, and three sets of networks and aggregators (insurance and financial services, home services, and legal).

For each entry: a name, the primary vertical, a one-sentence "known for," a one-paragraph description, and a public source to learn more.

The course does not maintain commercial relationships with any of the operators listed and is not paid by any of them. Names and product details are current as of May 2026; verify before relying on any specific claim in this reference.

Lead distribution platforms

The software layer that receives, filters, scores, and routes leads from supply-side to demand-side. Operators in this layer typically do not own consumer-facing brands or carry inventory risk; they sell software and infrastructure to networks, aggregators, and large generators.

1. LeadConduit (ActiveProspect)

Primary vertical

Cross-vertical infrastructure

Known for

The dominant lead distribution platform in the U.S. industry. Receives leads from many sources, applies filtering, enhancement, and validation steps, and routes leads to buyers under contracted rules.

Learn more

activeprospect.com — public product documentation and case studies.

Owned by ActiveProspect (Austin, TX), LeadConduit is the platform of record for a substantial share of mid-size and large lead operations across insurance, home services, legal, and financial services. Its primary functions are real-time filtering (DNC, duplicates, validation), enhancement (appending data from third-party sources), and routing (sending leads to buyers based on geographic, demographic, and contractual criteria). ActiveProspect's TrustedForm product, the most widely used consent capture certificate, integrates natively with LeadConduit. Most newcomers will encounter LeadConduit as the system their first network or aggregator buyer uses to receive leads.

2. Boberdoo

Primary vertical

Cross-vertical infrastructure

Known for

A long-running lead distribution platform popular with mid-size networks across insurance, home services, and legal.

Learn more

boberdoo.com — public documentation and product pages.

Boberdoo has operated since the mid-2000s as a workhorse platform for lead distribution. Compared with LeadConduit, Boberdoo is more commonly deployed by mid-size networks and brokers running their own buyer panels — operators who want flexibility in routing rules, ping-post auctions, and integration with their own websites without the complexity of an enterprise platform. Functional scope overlaps substantially with LeadConduit: filtering, routing, dispute and return handling, and integration with consent capture vendors. The competitive choice between Boberdoo and LeadConduit is usually a function of operator scale, integration needs, and price posture.

3. Phonexa

Primary vertical

Cross-vertical infrastructure (call-heavy)

Known for

An end-to-end performance marketing platform with strengths in call routing, attribution, lead distribution, and CRM.

Learn more

phonexa.com — public product documentation.

Phonexa positions itself as a unified performance marketing platform rather than a pure lead distribution tool. Strength is most pronounced in pay-per-call workflows — Phonexa's call routing, IVR, and attribution stack is competitive with the specialist call platforms — while also offering form lead distribution and CRM features. Operators running blended form-and-call inventory often choose Phonexa to keep their tooling stack consolidated. Particular share in insurance (especially Medicare during AEP), financial services, and home services.

4. Leadspedia

Primary vertical

Affiliate-flavored distribution & CRM

Known for

A lead distribution and CRM platform popular with affiliate networks.

Learn more

leadspedia.com — public documentation.

Leadspedia's product is closer in flavor to an affiliate-network-style tracking and routing stack — strong on publisher (affiliate) management, real-time ping-post auction logic, and the kind of attribution affiliate operators care about. Common in financial services and insurance verticals where affiliate-driven lead supply is meaningful. Competes with LeadConduit and Boberdoo in distribution and with affiliate-tracking platforms like CAKE in publisher management.

Consent capture & verification

Vendors that produce tamper-evident certificates of the consent a consumer gave at the moment of form submission or call connect. These products are increasingly contractually required by sophisticated buyers as a defense posture against TCPA claims.

5. TrustedForm (ActiveProspect)

Primary vertical

Cross-vertical compliance infrastructure

Known for

The dominant consent capture certificate. Embedded on a generator's form page, captures a record of the consumer's session and the consent disclosure language they saw, time-stamped and tamper-evident.

Learn more

activeprospect.com/products/trustedform — public product page.

TrustedForm produces a certificate identified by a unique URL that buyers can retain as evidence of compliant consent capture. The certificate covers the disclosure language the consumer saw, the page they saw it on, mouse and keystroke metadata, and the named parties whose calls and texts the consumer authorized. As of 2026 most large insurance and legal lead buyers contractually require a TrustedForm certificate (or equivalent) on every delivered lead. The product was a major factor in shifting the industry from "trust the generator" consent records to certified, third-party-verified consent records during the late 2010s.

6. Jornaya (Verisk Marketing Solutions)

Primary vertical

Cross-vertical compliance infrastructure

Known for

Consent capture and verification, equivalent in function to TrustedForm. Originally LeadiD; acquired by Verisk in 2018 and renamed Jornaya.

Learn more

jornaya.com — public product information.

Jornaya operates as a major alternative to TrustedForm and is the standard consent verification vendor used by several large insurance and financial services networks. Functionally similar: a certificate identifying the consent event, the disclosure language, and the named parties consented to. After Verisk's acquisition, Jornaya has become more integrated with Verisk's broader marketing data and risk products. Many large buyers will accept either Jornaya or TrustedForm; some require both; some have a vendor preference and will not buy leads without their preferred certificate.

Call routing & tracking

Platforms used by pay-per-call operators and any operator delivering qualified phone leads. Provide dynamic number insertion, IVR, call routing, recording (subject to state consent laws), and attribution.

7. Ringba

Primary vertical

Pay-per-call infrastructure

Known for

A widely used pay-per-call routing and tracking platform; particularly common in insurance pay-per-call (Medicare, ACA, auto), home services, and legal.

Learn more

ringba.com — public product documentation.

Ringba's product is built for the pay-per-call lifecycle: assign dynamic numbers, route calls through IVR or live agents, qualify against buyer-specific criteria, transfer qualified calls to the buyer, and bill per qualified connect. It competes with Phonexa (which is broader) and Retreaver (which is more enterprise-flavored). Many small and mid-size pay-per-call operators run their entire infrastructure on Ringba.

8. Retreaver

Primary vertical

Pay-per-call infrastructure

Known for

Pay-per-call routing and attribution platform, with stronger enterprise-buyer ergonomics than the smaller pay-per-call platforms.

Learn more

retreaver.com — public product information.

Retreaver's product is competitive with Ringba on core call routing but is often chosen by larger pay-per-call operations that need more sophisticated reporting, custom IVR design, and CRM integrations. Substantial share in financial services pay-per-call and in large insurance operations.

9. CallRail

Primary vertical

Call tracking & attribution (broader marketing)

Known for

Dynamic number insertion and call tracking for marketing attribution. Used widely by agencies and direct advertisers in addition to lead-gen operators.

Learn more

callrail.com — public product documentation.

CallRail is the call tracking product most commonly used by mid-size service businesses and the agencies that serve them. In lead generation, CallRail shows up most often on the advertiser side — buyers use it to attribute calls back to specific lead vendors and ad sources. Lead generators occasionally use CallRail for top-of-funnel attribution, though Ringba and Retreaver are more common when calls are the deliverable.

10. Invoca

Primary vertical

Enterprise call tracking & analytics

Known for

Enterprise-grade call tracking, attribution, and conversation analytics. Common at large advertisers (carriers, large law firms, multi-location franchises).

Learn more

invoca.com — public product information.

Invoca operates further up-market than CallRail, with stronger conversation analytics (transcription, intent classification, automated quality scoring) and tighter integrations with enterprise marketing platforms and CRMs. New operators will see Invoca more often referenced by enterprise advertisers than by lead-gen suppliers themselves.

Networks & aggregators — insurance and financial services

The large operators in the auto, home, life, health, Medicare, and consumer-finance lead categories. Each owns one or more consumer-facing brands and supplies large numbers of carriers and agents.

11. QuinStreet (NASDAQ: QNST)

Primary vertical

Insurance, consumer finance

Known for

Publicly traded operator of consumer-facing insurance comparison brands (including NextGen Leads and Modern Health), plus a substantial consumer-finance lead generation business.

Learn more

investor.quinstreet.com — public filings, including 10-K and quarterly earnings.

QuinStreet has been a public-market constant in the insurance lead generation space since its 2010 IPO. Operates a portfolio of consumer-facing brands across auto and home insurance, Medicare, and consumer finance, and supplies a substantial share of the carrier and agent demand in those verticals. QuinStreet's 10-K and earnings call commentary is a useful primary source for anyone trying to understand insurance-vertical unit economics at scale. The company's results are highly sensitive to carrier marketing budgets and to the rate environment in mortgage refinance.

12. MediaAlpha (NYSE: MAX)

Primary vertical

Insurance, consumer finance, travel, health

Known for

Publicly traded operator of an exchange-style platform connecting performance marketing supply with consumer-finance and insurance demand at scale.

Learn more

investors.mediaalpha.com — public filings.

MediaAlpha's primary business is a programmatic exchange that matches insurance and consumer-finance traffic supply (publishers and media buyers) with carrier and agent demand on a per-click and per-call basis. The company's IPO and subsequent disclosures provided the most detailed public window into the segment-level economics of insurance lead generation, including the impact of carrier underwriting cycles on demand. MediaAlpha's results have been notably volatile, reflecting the underwriting cycle's effect on carrier marketing spend.

13. EverQuote (NASDAQ: EVER)

Primary vertical

Auto, home, life insurance

Known for

Publicly traded consumer-facing insurance comparison brand and lead provider, with a substantial own-and-operate funnel as well as a wholesale lead business.

Learn more

investors.everquote.com — public filings.

EverQuote operates everquote.com as a consumer-facing insurance comparison site and supplies leads to carriers and agents at scale. Like QuinStreet and MediaAlpha, EverQuote's public filings provide useful segment-level transparency into auto and home insurance lead economics. The company's results are sensitive to carrier marketing spend, which contracts in periods of carrier underwriting losses (as it did in 2022–2023).

14. Hometown Quotes

Primary vertical

Insurance

Known for

Long-running independent insurance lead provider and aggregator, particularly active in auto and home insurance.

Learn more

hometownquotes.com — public information.

Hometown Quotes has operated as a privately held insurance lead provider since the early 2000s and has built a reputation in agent-facing channels for working with small and mid-size agencies that don't have the procurement sophistication of national carriers. Often named in trade conversations as a starter-friendly demand-side relationship for newer brokers and generators, though terms and standards still apply.

15. SmartFinancial

Primary vertical

Insurance

Known for

Insurance lead and call provider, with consumer-facing comparison brand operations.

Learn more

smartfinancial.com — public site and partner pages.

SmartFinancial operates as a mid-to-large insurance lead provider with a particular profile in auto insurance comparison-funnel inventory and Medicare. Common counterparty for agencies buying in moderate volume and for affiliates supplying insurance shopping traffic.

Networks & aggregators — home services

The dominant operators in roofing, HVAC, plumbing, solar, remodeling, and adjacent home-services verticals. Many own homeowner-facing consumer brands and supply contractor demand at national scale.

16. Angi Leads (formerly HomeAdvisor) — Angi Inc. (NASDAQ: ANGI)

Primary vertical

Home services

Known for

The largest U.S. home-services demand operator. Acquires homeowner project requests through its consumer brand and supplies contractor demand on a per-lead basis.

Learn more

ir.angi.com — public filings.

HomeAdvisor was the long-standing brand; Angi consolidated the consumer-facing brand and underlying lead delivery in the early 2020s. As of 2026 Angi Inc. remains publicly traded and continues to operate the largest demand-aggregation footprint in home services. Many contractors and franchises buy primarily or partly from Angi; many home-services-focused brokers and generators source overflow inventory there as well. Angi's public filings (10-K, quarterly earnings) are a useful primary source for home-services lead economics at scale.

17. Modernize

Primary vertical

Home services (remodeling-heavy)

Known for

Consumer-facing home-improvement lead aggregator, with particular share in window, siding, roofing, HVAC, and remodeling.

Learn more

modernize.com — public information.

Modernize operates a consumer-facing portfolio of home-improvement comparison and lead-capture sites and supplies contractor demand at scale. Acquired by QuinStreet in 2018 and operated within QuinStreet's portfolio. Common counterparty for mid-size and large contractor operations; less commonly accessed by very small contractors due to volume minimums.

18. Networx

Primary vertical

Home services

Known for

Home-services lead aggregator with broad trade coverage (HVAC, plumbing, roofing, electrical, remodeling, cleaning).

Learn more

networx.com — public information.

Networx has operated as a home-services lead provider since the early 2010s, with a contractor-facing model that emphasizes per-lead pricing and credit-based dispute handling. Frequently named alongside Angi and Modernize as one of the three demand-side operators a contractor will be asked to evaluate. Smaller than Angi by total volume but with meaningful share in specific trades and geographies.

Networks & aggregators — legal

Specialist operators in personal injury, mass tort, and other legal verticals. Legal lead generation is heavily regulated under state bar advertising rules; established networks have built their compliance posture accordingly.

19. Legal Brand Marketing

Primary vertical

Legal — personal injury, mass tort, criminal defense

Known for

Multi-vertical legal lead provider with substantial share in personal injury and mass tort, plus other practice areas.

Learn more

legalbrandmarketing.com — public site.

Legal Brand Marketing (LBM) has operated as a specialist legal lead provider since the mid-2010s. Strength is most pronounced in personal injury and mass tort verticals, where LBM's combination of paid media generation and intake handling has produced the kind of signed-case volume that mid-size and larger PI firms buy in bulk. LBM is frequently named in trade conversations as the established demand-side relationship for brokers attempting to enter the legal vertical from the supply side.

20. 4LegalLeads

Primary vertical

Legal — broad practice-area coverage

Known for

Long-running legal lead provider with broad practice-area coverage and a substantial small-and-solo attorney customer base.

Learn more

4legalleads.com — public site.

4LegalLeads has operated since the late 2000s as a legal lead aggregator covering personal injury, family law, bankruptcy, criminal defense, immigration, and other consumer-facing legal practice areas. The customer base skews toward solo and small-firm attorneys who buy in low-to-moderate volume. Often mentioned in operator conversations as one of the more accessible demand-side relationships for newer legal lead operators.

21. ngage Live Chat / nGen

Primary vertical

Legal — intake conversion infrastructure

Known for

Live-chat and call-intake infrastructure for law firms. Adjacent to lead generation rather than within it, but consistently named in legal-lead conversations because it sits between the lead and the signed case.

Learn more

ngagelive.com — public product information.

ngage operates a live-chat product widely deployed on law firm websites to capture and qualify visitor inquiries in real time. The product is adjacent to lead generation in the strict sense but routinely appears in trade conversations because intake conversion (the rate at which a delivered lead becomes a signed case) is the primary driver of buyer-side CAC, and ngage is one of the most-named tools for improving that conversion in legal verticals.

Not in this list (and why)

Several operators that show up in trade conversations are intentionally not in this 20: small specialist brokers in narrow sub-verticals (a list of those is more useful in Module 2, where vertical selection is the topic); operators whose product and corporate status changed materially in the months immediately preceding the May 2026 cutoff for this version; and operators whose primary business is in adjacent industries (general marketing analytics, broader CRM) rather than lead generation specifically. The list will be re-evaluated each quarter.

If an operator that is or should be major in your target vertical isn't covered here, please flag it via the feedback link in the course resource library. The next quarterly revision is built around exactly that input.
