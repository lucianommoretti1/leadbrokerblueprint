Major Buyers & Networks

Organized by vertical

How to use this reference

For each major vertical: who actually buys leads (demand side), who supplies them at scale (supply side), where the buyers congregate (conferences, associations, communities), and notes on beginner accessibility.

This complements Module 1's deliverable, which was organized by player category. Together they give you both views — which operators exist (Module 1), and where to look in each vertical (this document).

Not an endorsement. No commercial relationship between the course and any operator listed. Current as of May 2026; verify before using in a sales conversation.

Legal services

Personal injury is the highest-priced sub-vertical; family law, bankruptcy, and immigration are the most newcomer-accessible.

Demand side — who buys

Personal injury law firms

Solo and small-firm PI attorneys buy in low volume; mid-size firms (10–50 attorneys) are the workhorse segment for shared and exclusive form leads; large PI firms and intake-machine litigators (e.g., Morgan & Morgan-scale) buy at procurement-team scale.

Mass tort plaintiff firms

Specialist firms that aggregate plaintiffs for MDL litigation; purchase signed retainers at $1,500–$5,000+. Names appear in Mass Torts Made Perfect conference materials.

Family / bankruptcy / immigration firms

Mostly solo and small-firm; lower per-lead prices but accessible buyer pools for newcomers.

Supply side — major operators

Legal Brand Marketing

Multi-vertical legal lead provider; strong in PI and mass tort.

4LegalLeads

Broad practice-area legal lead aggregator; accessible to small attorneys.

X Social Media

Mass-tort and PI lead generation specialist; large paid-social and intake footprint.

ngage Live / nGen

Live-chat intake infrastructure for law firm sites; adjacent to lead gen but often discussed alongside.

Where buyers congregate

  • Mass Torts Made Perfect (MTMP) — major mass-tort plaintiff-firm conference.
  • LeadsCon — general lead-gen conference with substantial legal track.
  • ABA TECHSHOW and state-bar marketing events — for attorney-side perspective.
  • Reddit: r/PIlawyers, r/Lawyertalk (operator perspective is limited here; useful for understanding the buyer mindset).

Newcomer accessibility note

Family law, bankruptcy, and immigration are the realistic newcomer entry points. PI is high-ceiling but capital-intensive and procurement-heavy; mass tort requires specialist relationships.

Home services

Demand concentration in three large operators (Angi, Modernize, Networx); newcomer entry generally easier in pest control, plumbing, and small-trade remodeling than in roofing/HVAC.

Demand side — who buys

National franchise systems

ARS/Rescue Rooter (HVAC/plumbing), Roto-Rooter (plumbing), national HVAC franchise systems. Centralized procurement; larger volume but harder to access for new vendors.

Regional contractor chains

5–25-location HVAC and roofing operations; the workhorse buyer segment in their geographies.

Independent contractors

Solo and 2–5-person operations; lower per-lead prices, fragmented procurement, accessible to newcomers especially when the broker has trade-network relationships.

Supply side — major operators

Angi (formerly HomeAdvisor)

Largest home-services demand aggregator. Public on NASDAQ as ANGI.

Modernize

Home-improvement comparison and lead aggregator; part of QuinStreet.

Networx

Multi-trade home-services lead provider.

CraftJack

Trade-focused lead aggregator (Angi-owned in part).

HomeYou

Remodeling and home-improvement leads.

Local generator brands

Many regional brokers; surface through local-SEO competitive analysis.

Where buyers congregate

  • Service World Expo — major HVAC/plumbing contractor conference (buyer side).
  • International Roofing Expo — major roofing contractor conference.
  • Service Roundtable, Nexstar Network — contractor associations and buying groups.
  • PHCC (Plumbing-Heating-Cooling Contractors) — trade association.

Newcomer accessibility note

Pest control, plumbing, cleaning, and small-trade remodeling are the more newcomer-accessible sub-verticals. Roofing and HVAC have higher CPLs but more concentrated buyer-side competition.

Insurance

Largest vertical, highest competition. Entry without an insurance license is largely blocked; agent-direct and Medicare-AEP windows are the most accessible starter paths.

Demand side — who buys

National carriers

Progressive, GEICO, State Farm, Allstate, Liberty Mutual, etc. Buy at procurement-team scale; new-vendor approval is rigorous and lengthy.

Captive and IMO/FMO agents

Insurance Marketing Organizations / Field Marketing Organizations aggregate agent demand and resell leads.

Independent agencies

Small and mid-size agencies; lower-volume buyers, accessible to new vendors at modest scale.

Supply side — major operators

MediaAlpha (NYSE: MAX)

Insurance and consumer-finance programmatic exchange. Public filings disclose segment economics.

QuinStreet (NASDAQ: QNST)

Operates consumer-facing insurance brands (NextGen Leads, Modern Health).

EverQuote (NASDAQ: EVER)

Consumer-facing auto/home/life insurance comparison brand.

AllWebLeads

Long-running insurance lead provider; auto-heavy.

Datalot

Insurance pay-per-call specialist; carrier and agent demand.

SmartFinancial

Insurance lead and call provider.

Hometown Quotes

Independent insurance lead provider; accessible to small agencies.

TZ Insurance Solutions (TogetherHealth)

Medicare-focused lead and call provider.

Where buyers congregate

  • LeadsCon — substantial insurance track.
  • ITA Insurance Marketing & Tech Summit.
  • NAIFA (National Association of Insurance and Financial Advisors) chapter events.
  • Carrier-specific marketing conferences (Allstate, State Farm, etc., for captive-channel perspective).

Newcomer accessibility note

Generally a hard beginner vertical. Workable paths: agent-direct sales to independent agencies; Medicare during AEP (Oct 15 – Dec 7) if the operator is licensed or partnered with a licensed entity; final expense / small face-value life with smaller agent buyer pool.

Financial services

Heterogeneous vertical; mortgage is largest and most cyclical; debt relief, tax resolution, credit repair carry significant CFPB / state AG enforcement risk.

Demand side — who buys

Mortgage lenders and brokers

Loan officers and broker shops; demand collapses in high-rate environments (refinance) and recovers with purchase volume.

Debt-settlement law firms

Small number of large back-end firms that buy debt-relief inquiries at scale.

Tax-resolution firms

Optima Tax Relief and other large operators; smaller regional firms; FTC and state AG enforcement has been active.

Business loan / MCA funders

Merchant cash advance and small-business finance providers.

Supply side — major operators

LendingTree (NASDAQ: TREE)

Largest consumer-finance comparison platform.

Bankrate / Red Ventures

Consumer-finance content + lead generation; broad.

Modern Health (QuinStreet)

Consumer finance lead aggregation under the QuinStreet umbrella.

Specialty operators

Tax resolution and credit repair sub-verticals have many smaller operators; few dominant brands.

Where buyers congregate

  • Mortgage Bankers Association (MBA) annual conference.
  • LeadsCon (financial services track).
  • NACBA (debt-settlement industry association).
  • Inc. 5000 / SMB Tech conferences for business-loan dynamics.

Newcomer accessibility note

Generally rewards operators with existing licensure (NMLS for mortgage) or domain expertise. Tax resolution and credit repair are workable for newcomers with deliberate compliance posture; debt relief is harder due to concentrated buyer pool.

Healthcare and medical

Cash-pay sub-verticals (med spa, dental, cosmetic, weight-loss) are the accessible side; insurance-billing clinical practices come with HIPAA constraints; addiction treatment is a specialized EKRA category.

Demand side — who buys

Med spa & aesthetics practices

Cash-pay; fragmented; accessible buyer pool.

Dental practices

Large dental DSOs (Heartland Dental, Aspen Dental, Western Dental) plus many independent practices.

Cosmetic surgery practices

Solo and small-group practices; cash-pay; receptive to lead vendors.

Weight-loss / GLP-1 telehealth

Newer category; rapidly growing; programs include Ro, Hims & Hers, Noom, Calibrate.

Senior care / home health agencies

A Place for Mom dominates senior placement; home-health is more fragmented.

Addiction treatment centers

EKRA + state regulations heavily constrain marketing compensation.

Supply side — major operators

A Place for Mom

Dominant senior-living placement lead generator and referral source.

1-800-DENTIST

Long-running dental lead provider.

Modernizing Medicine / network of practice-specific lead generators

Most healthcare lead supply is fragmented and practice-vertical-specific.

Hims & Hers / Ro / Noom

Consumer-facing telehealth and weight-loss platforms (also have B2B/affiliate channels).

Where buyers congregate

  • American Med Spa Association annual conference.
  • ADA SmileCon — American Dental Association.
  • American Academy of Cosmetic Surgery annual meeting.
  • Argentum (senior living industry association).

Newcomer accessibility note

Med spa and dental are the most accessible newcomer entry points. Addiction treatment requires specialist EKRA-compliant structuring; insurance-billing clinical practices require deliberate HIPAA scoping.

B2B services

Operationally distinct from consumer; longer cycles, committee buying, outbound and LinkedIn dominate. Looks more like agency work than consumer brokering.

Demand side — who buys

B2B SaaS companies

Buying MQLs and SQMs; sophisticated procurement; ICP-specific demand.

IT and managed services providers

Fragmented; smaller per-buyer volume.

Accounting and bookkeeping firms

Solo and small-firm buyers; receptive to specialty lead vendors.

B2B financial services / fintech

Mid-to-large buyers; ICP-specific demand.

Commercial real estate / brokers

Geographic and asset-class-specific demand.

Supply side — major operators

LinkedIn (paid social)

Dominant paid channel; complex but capable.

Cognism, Apollo, ZoomInfo

B2B data and outbound infrastructure (operator-side).

G2, Capterra, TrustRadius

SaaS demand-gen via review platforms.

DemandScience, MarketStar, Belkins

B2B demand-gen and SDR-style service providers (agency-flavored).

Where buyers congregate

  • SaaStr — SaaS industry conference.
  • Pavilion (community / private membership for B2B revenue leaders).
  • Demand Gen Summit (BrightTALK).
  • Sales Hacker / Outreach annual events.

Newcomer accessibility note

Easier for operators with existing B2B sales experience; harder for operators from consumer marketing backgrounds. Outbound and LinkedIn are different skills from paid search/social.

Local services & other consumer

Long tail of accessible niches. Often the second sub-vertical for an operator running something larger.

Demand side — who buys

Real estate agents

Buyer-lead demand dominated by Zillow Premier Agent and Realtor.com Connections Plus.

Cash-buyer / iBuyer programs

We Buy Houses, Opendoor (intermittent), Offerpad, regional cash-buyer franchises.

Dealerships

Auto-shopping leads; concentrated buyer side.

Schools & training programs

Career schools, online degree programs, K-12 tutoring services.

Movers, locksmiths, etc.

Long tail of local-service buyers.

Supply side — major operators

Zillow / Realtor.com

Dominant real-estate-lead supply.

CarsForSale, TrueCar, Edmunds, Cars.com

Auto shopping lead supply.

Affiliate-driven specialty operators

Many small operators in education, moving, and similar niches.

Where buyers congregate

  • Inman Connect — real estate industry conference.
  • NADA Show — National Automobile Dealers Association.
  • Local-vertical Reddit and Facebook contractor groups.

Newcomer accessibility note

Most accessible categories for a newcomer with relevant local relationships. Rarely large enough to be a scaled standalone business but useful as a starter.
