Compliance Checklist

Module 3 deliverable

← Module 3 overview Download PDF

Six sections. ~60 items. Each cites the regulation it satisfies.

How to use this checklist

Work through every applicable item before launch. For each item:

• Check the box only when you can produce the artifact named in the right column.

• Save the artifact and tag it with the item it satisfies.

• Re-verify quarterly. Regulations change; the course publishes a refreshed checklist each quarter.

Section 5 is vertical-specific. Apply only the rows for your vertical. Treat the rest of Section 5 as informational background.

This checklist is the operational floor; it is not legal advice for your specific situation. Counsel familiar with your vertical and operating states is the next step beyond the checklist.

Section 1 — Telephony & texting (TCPA, DNC, TSR)

The single largest source of litigation exposure for lead generators. Work through every item before you place a single marketing call or send a single marketing text.

☐ Every marketing call or text to a mobile number captures Prior Express Written Consent (PEWC) before contact.

Regulation: TCPA / 47 C.F.R. § 64.1200(f)(9).

Artifact: Form disclosure language; signed consent record; TrustedForm or Jornaya certificate URL.

Source: 47 U.S.C. § 227; 47 C.F.R. § 64.1200.

☐ Consent disclosure satisfies all four PEWC elements: clear and conspicuous; authorizes autodialer/prerecorded contact; states consent is not a condition of purchase; consumer's signature.

Regulation: 47 C.F.R. § 64.1200(f)(9) (four elements).

Artifact: Page screenshots; legal review note; explicit checkbox-and-signature design.

Source: 47 C.F.R. § 64.1200(f)(9).

☐ PEWC disclosures name the specific seller(s) to whom calls/texts are authorized. Single-seller (one-to-one) format used where buyer contracts require it.

Regulation: Post-IMC v. FCC; multi-seller permitted as regulatory matter; buyer contract often stricter.

Artifact: Disclosure language; vendor-side validation that named parties match buyer contracts.

Source: Insurance Marketing Coalition v. FCC, No. 24-10277 (11th Cir. Jan. 24, 2025).

☐ Revocation handling: any reasonable means (STOP, email, verbal) is honored within 10 business days; no exclusive channel required.

Regulation: FCC Revocation Rule, effective April 11, 2025.

Artifact: Documented revocation channels; CRM workflow ticket showing propagation within 10 BD.

Source: FCC Final Rule, CG Docket 02-278 (Feb. 15, 2024; effective April 11, 2025).

☐ Confirmation-text safe harbor: at most one non-marketing confirmation text within 5 minutes of revocation receipt.

Regulation: FCC Revocation Rule (5-minute safe harbor).

Artifact: Confirmation-message template; STOP-keyword routing config.

Source: FCC Final Rule, CG Docket 02-278.

☐ Revocation propagates across all downstream parties (every buyer of the lead, every seller in the consent chain).

Regulation: FCC Revocation Rule (scope-of-revocation).

Artifact: Lead distribution platform's revocation-write-back mechanism (LeadConduit or equivalent); audit log.

Source: FCC Final Rule, CG Docket 02-278.

☐ National DNC Registry scrubbed within the regulators' timing window (≤31 days) before any campaign call or text.

Regulation: TCPA + TSR.

Artifact: Scrubbing-vendor contract; scrub-date timestamps; campaign-level proof.

Source: 16 C.F.R. § 310; 47 C.F.R. § 64.1200(c).

☐ STOP responses are treated both as consent revocation and as a DNC-equivalent signal across voice and text.

Regulation: FCC TCPA rules; FCC text-message orders (2023).

Artifact: STOP-handling workflow that suppresses all future marketing contact, voice or text.

Source: 47 C.F.R. § 64.1200; FCC orders on text-message DNC.

☐ Predictive-dialer abandoned-call rate under the TSR safe harbor (3% over 30-day per-campaign rolling window).

Regulation: TSR § 310.4(b)(4).

Artifact: Dialer abandonment metrics; campaign-level monitoring; live-agent connection logs.

Source: 16 C.F.R. § 310.4(b)(4).

☐ Caller ID accurately transmitted on outbound calls (seller name or telemarketer name).

Regulation: TSR § 310.4(a)(8).

Artifact: Dialer configuration; Caller ID validation.

Source: 16 C.F.R. § 310.4(a)(8).

☐ Five-year record retention for TSR-required records (ads, employee names, sales records, authorizations).

Regulation: TSR § 310.5.

Artifact: Document retention policy with five-year minimum; storage location and access controls.

Source: 16 C.F.R. § 310.5.

Section 2 — Advertising content (FTC Endorsement Guides + Reviews Rule + Section 5)

Every landing page, email, social ad, and operator-facing sales page is in scope. The October 2024 Reviews Rule has put fake or unsubstantiated content directly at civil-penalty risk.

☐ No fake or AI-generated reviews or testimonials anywhere in the marketing stack.

Regulation: FTC Reviews Rule (effective Oct. 21, 2024).

Artifact: Audit log; landing-page inventory with confirmed-real-reviewer records.

Source: 16 C.F.R. Part 465.

☐ No buying or selling of reviews (positive or negative; cash or non-cash compensation).

Regulation: FTC Reviews Rule.

Artifact: Policy statement; vendor agreements prohibiting paid-review acquisition.

Source: 16 C.F.R. Part 465.

☐ No insider reviews (employees, agents, family) without clear material-connection disclosure.

Regulation: FTC Reviews Rule; Endorsement Guides.

Artifact: Internal policy; review-source audit.

Source: 16 C.F.R. Part 465; 16 C.F.R. § 255.5.

☐ No review suppression — no threats of legal action to remove honest negative reviews.

Regulation: FTC Reviews Rule.

Artifact: Internal escalation policy; documentation of how negative reviews are handled.

Source: 16 C.F.R. Part 465.

☐ No advertiser-controlled "independent" review sites; if you run a review-style domain, its ownership is disclosed.

Regulation: FTC Reviews Rule.

Artifact: Domain ownership disclosures on any owned review-style site.

Source: 16 C.F.R. Part 465.

☐ No fake social-media metrics (bought followers, likes, engagement).

Regulation: FTC Reviews Rule.

Artifact: Social-media auditor results; vendor agreements.

Source: 16 C.F.R. Part 465.

☐ Material connections to endorsers (payments, free products, employment, family) are clearly and conspicuously disclosed.

Regulation: FTC Endorsement Guides (revised June 2023).

Artifact: Disclosure language; influencer-contract template; pre-publication review process.

Source: 16 C.F.R. Part 255.

☐ No "results not typical" boilerplate where atypical results are featured; substantive typical-result context provided.

Regulation: FTC Endorsement Guides.

Artifact: Marketing-claim substantiation file; typical-results data.

Source: 16 C.F.R. Part 255.

☐ Every advertising claim has adequate substantiation on file.

Regulation: FTC Act § 5 (15 U.S.C. § 45).

Artifact: Claim-substantiation file mapping claims to evidence.

Source: FTC Act § 5; FTC Policy Statement on Advertising Substantiation.

Section 3 — Email (CAN-SPAM)

Email is the least-strict regime among the federal marketing-channel rules, but the five core requirements still apply to every commercial email.

☐ Accurate "from" and "reply-to" header information identifying the sender.

Regulation: CAN-SPAM § 7704(a)(1).

Artifact: Email-platform sender configuration; SPF / DKIM / DMARC alignment.

Source: 15 U.S.C. § 7704.

☐ Non-deceptive subject lines.

Regulation: CAN-SPAM § 7704(a)(2).

Artifact: Subject-line review process; pre-send approval workflow for promotional emails.

Source: 15 U.S.C. § 7704.

☐ Commercial emails identified as advertisements (explicitly if not obvious from content).

Regulation: CAN-SPAM § 7704(a)(5).

Artifact: Email template inclusion of advertisement-identification language.

Source: 15 U.S.C. § 7704.

☐ Every commercial email includes a valid physical postal address of the sender.

Regulation: CAN-SPAM § 7704(a)(5).

Artifact: Email-template audit; current registered business address on file.

Source: 15 U.S.C. § 7704.

☐ Clear opt-out mechanism honored within 10 business days; opt-outs respected for at least 30 days post-message.

Regulation: CAN-SPAM § 7704(a)(3)–(4).

Artifact: Unsubscribe link in every email; ESP unsubscribe-list propagation; 30-day-honor policy.

Source: 15 U.S.C. § 7704.

Section 4 — Privacy (state laws, FCRA touchpoints, data-broker registration)

Build to the California standard unless you have a specific reason not to. Re-verify quarterly — new state laws keep going into effect.

☐ Public privacy notice covers California CCPA/CPRA disclosure requirements (categories of data collected, purposes, sale, retention, consumer rights).

Regulation: CCPA/CPRA, Cal. Civ. Code § 1798.100 et seq.

Artifact: Privacy notice on every consumer-facing site; legal review.

Source: Cal. Civ. Code § 1798.100 et seq.

☐ Lead-data transfers to buyers / aggregators recognized and disclosed as "sale of personal information" in CCPA and other state notices.

Regulation: CCPA "sale" definition; equivalent state-law sale definitions.

Artifact: Notice language; sale-flow documentation.

Source: Cal. Civ. Code § 1798.140(t).

☐ Consumer rights honored (access, deletion, correction, opt-out of sale / targeted advertising) across all states with applicable laws.

Regulation: CCPA, VCDPA, CPA, CTDPA, UCPA, TDPSA, and successor state laws.

Artifact: Privacy-request intake workflow; response-time SLA; recordkeeping.

Source: State privacy statutes (see citation reference sheet).

☐ Global Privacy Control (GPC) signals honored as opt-out-of-sale requests where applicable.

Regulation: CCPA/CPRA regulations; equivalent state rules.

Artifact: Site-side GPC detection; documented opt-out propagation.

Source: Cal. Code Regs. tit. 11, § 7025 (CPPA regulations on GPC).

☐ Data-broker registration completed in California, Texas, Vermont, and Oregon if the operator meets each state's definition.

Regulation: State data-broker registration statutes.

Artifact: Annual registrations; metric submissions where required.

Source: Cal. Civ. Code § 1798.99.80; Tex. Bus. & Com. Code Ch. 509; Vt. Stat. tit. 9 § 2430; Or. Rev. Stat. § 646A.602.

☐ Reasonable security measures and breach-notification process in place for personal information held.

Regulation: State privacy laws + state breach-notification laws.

Artifact: Information-security policy; incident-response plan; encryption at rest and in transit.

Source: State privacy and breach-notification statutes.

☐ No use of consumer-report data without an FCRA-permissible purpose; no purchases of "pre-screened" lead lists without diligence on source.

Regulation: Fair Credit Reporting Act.

Artifact: Vendor-diligence documentation; lead-source contractual reps.

Source: 15 U.S.C. § 1681 et seq.

Section 5 — Vertical-specific (apply only the rows for your vertical)

Map your vertical onto its specific regulatory layer. Treat the rest of this section as informational background.

☐ [LEGAL] Compensation structured as per-lead flat fees for advertising; not as percentage of recovery or contingent on outcome.

Regulation: ABA Model Rule 5.4; state bar variations.

Artifact: Lead purchase agreement structure; state-by-state bar review for attorney clients.

Source: ABA Model Rules of Professional Conduct, Rule 5.4.

☐ [LEGAL] Advertising representations comply with ABA Model Rules 7.2 (advertising) and 7.3 (solicitation), as adopted by the state of the attorney client.

Regulation: ABA Model Rules 7.2, 7.3.

Artifact: State-by-state review of marketing copy; bar-ethics-opinion file.

Source: ABA Model Rules 7.2, 7.3.

☐ [MORTGAGE] NMLS licensure verified for the lead provider in every state where activity requires it.

Regulation: NMLS state licensing.

Artifact: Active NMLS registrations; renewals tracked.

Source: Nationwide Multistate Licensing System; state mortgage codes.

☐ [MORTGAGE] Pay-per-lead compensation structured as reasonable payment for marketing services rendered; not contingent on funding or as a percentage of loan amount.

Regulation: RESPA Section 8; CFPB Bulletin 2015-05.

Artifact: Marketing services agreement; documented service deliverables; reasonable-value analysis.

Source: 12 U.S.C. § 2607; CFPB Bulletin 2015-05.

☐ [INSURANCE] State producer (agent) licensing held for any activity that crosses into solicitation or negotiation of insurance.

Regulation: State insurance codes; NAIC Producer Licensing Model Act.

Artifact: Active state producer licenses; CE compliance.

Source: State insurance department licensing requirements.

☐ [INSURANCE] Compensation to non-licensed marketers does not violate state anti-rebating laws.

Regulation: State anti-rebating statutes; NAIC commission-splitting rules.

Artifact: Compensation structure documented; legal-review opinion.

Source: State insurance codes (vary by state).

☐ [INSURANCE-MEDICARE] CMS Medicare Communications and Marketing Guidelines complied with — scope of appointment, recording, prohibited claims about plan benefits.

Regulation: CMS MCMG (annual edition).

Artifact: Agent training records; SOA documentation; recording retention.

Source: 42 C.F.R. § 422.2260 et seq.; CMS Medicare Communications & Marketing Guidelines.

☐ [HEALTHCARE] HIPAA-compliant authorization captured before any use of PHI for marketing purposes; otherwise no PHI captured.

Regulation: HIPAA Privacy Rule.

Artifact: Authorization form; data-handling policy that segregates PHI from non-PHI flows.

Source: 45 C.F.R. §§ 164.501, 164.508.

☐ [ADDICTION TREATMENT] No percentage-based or per-acquired-patient compensation to marketers; per-lead flat fees only, structured as advertising payments.

Regulation: EKRA, 18 U.S.C. § 220.

Artifact: Compensation structure documented; legal-review opinion.

Source: 18 U.S.C. § 220.

☐ [INVESTIGATIVE] State private-investigator licensing complied with by the operator and downstream parties.

Regulation: State PI licensing statutes.

Artifact: License registrations; downstream-party diligence.

Source: State PI licensing codes (vary by state).

Section 6 — Documentation & operations

The compliance posture has to leave evidence. If you cannot produce the artifacts under audit, you cannot defend the compliance posture in litigation.

☐ Consent records retained for at least five years; tamper-evident certificate URLs accessible per delivered lead.

Regulation: TCPA / TSR / general litigation-defense posture.

Artifact: Certificate-URL database; retention policy; sampling-and-audit process.

Source: 16 C.F.R. § 310.5 (TSR retention); operator best practice.

☐ Vendor and supplier contracts include reps and warranties on consent, source, and compliance.

Regulation: Cross-cutting (TCPA, FTC, state privacy).

Artifact: Master supply agreement template; signed counterparty agreements.

Source: Industry-standard contract clauses.

☐ Indemnification flows from lead-source to lead-provider to lead-buyer for breach-of-rep claims.

Regulation: Cross-cutting.

Artifact: Mutual indemnification clauses in supply and buyer contracts.

Source: Industry-standard contract clauses.

☐ Audit-trail evidence is preserved for at least three years for every operational step (lead capture, distribution, contact, revocation handling).

Regulation: Cross-cutting.

Artifact: Audit-log retention policy; logging at every workflow step.

Source: Operator best practice.

☐ Documented quarterly compliance review with sign-off; this checklist re-verified each quarter.

Regulation: Cross-cutting.

Artifact: Quarterly compliance review minutes; this checklist with verification dates.

Source: Course recommendation.

☐ Counsel relationship established for the operator's primary vertical and operating states.

Regulation: Cross-cutting.

Artifact: Engagement letter with vertical-specialist counsel.

Source: Operator best practice.
