Cold outreach, follow-up, objection handling, and negotiation

How to use these scripts

These are starting points, not final scripts. They reflect what operators publicly describe as effective patterns for new-vendor outreach to small and mid-size buyers. Modify them to your voice, your vertical, and your specific situation.

Three principles: be specific (vertical, geography, volume); be credible (consent infrastructure, compliance posture); be low-commitment in the ask (15-minute call, sample leads, evaluation period). Generic vendor pitches don't open conversations.

Script 1 — Cold email opener

Subject lines that work

Email body

Hi [First Name],

I run a small lead-generation operation focused on [specific sub-vertical, e.g., "family law in metro Atlanta"]. Producing [_____ leads / week] right now, exclusive form leads with TrustedForm consent capture and standard intake qualification.

I'm building out my buyer roster and looking for two or three starter customers in your area willing to evaluate a small batch. Happy to send the first [3-5] leads at no cost so you can compare intake conversion against your existing supply.

If they work for you, we can talk pricing and terms. If they don't, no obligation. Worth a 15-minute call this week or next to discuss?

[Your name]

[Phone, email, simple operator title]

Script 2 — Follow-up sequence

If the first email gets no response (the typical outcome), a short follow-up sequence:

Follow-up 1 (3-5 days after initial)

Hi [First Name],

Following up on the note I sent last week about [sub-vertical] leads in [metro]. Probably caught you at a busy time. Two questions if it's easier than a call:

1. Are you actively considering new lead vendors right now, or is your supply solid?

2. If you were considering, what would matter most to you — price, exclusivity, source mix, something else?

Either answer is useful. Thanks.

[Your name]

Follow-up 2 (5-7 days after follow-up 1)

Last note from me on this — I don't want to over-email. Couple of operators in your area have started using our leads recently in [sub-vertical]; happy to share a sample of the first ten leads we'd send your way (anonymized) if you'd like to evaluate quality before any conversation.

If now isn't the right time, I'll check back in a few months. Thanks.

[Your name]

Three follow-ups is usually the right cadence. Beyond that, you're closer to harassment than to sales. Move on; you can revisit in 4-6 months.

Script 3 — The first discovery call

Opening (90 seconds)

Thanks for taking the time, [Name]. Quick context on my end and then I want to learn about your operation.

I'm [your name]; I run [operation name], a [size description] lead-gen operation in [sub-vertical] focused on [geography]. We capture leads through [traffic source description] with [consent vendor] consent records. Currently producing [_____ leads / month]; supplying [_____ buyers] including [reference if you have one, otherwise skip].

What I'd like to understand from you today: what your current lead supply looks like, what's working, what isn't, and whether there's a fit. Mind if I ask a few questions?

Discovery questions

Listen more than you talk on a first call. The buyer's answers tell you everything you need to know about whether you can serve them, and the act of being listened to is itself a meaningful differentiator from generic vendor pitches.

Script 4 — Objection handling

"We're not looking for new vendors right now."

Understood. Quick question — if a current supplier had a quality issue or a sudden volume drop, would you want to have a vetted backup in place, or would you start evaluating from scratch at that moment?

I'm not asking for a contract today. Just asking whether it would be useful to have us as a vetted option you could turn on if you needed to. I can send a small sample so you have something to evaluate when timing is right.

"Your price is too high."

Help me understand. Compared to what — your shared inventory, or your exclusive inventory? Because we're priced at [exclusive / shared] tier and the comparison should be apples to apples.

If we are above your benchmark for the same lead type, what's the gap? Sometimes the gap is intake-conversion math — at our typical conversion rate, the CAC works out to [X]; what does your CAC look like at the vendors you're using today?

"We've been burned by new vendors before."

Fair concern. Most lead-gen operators that fail buyers are failing on one of three things — consent records, qualification rigor, or speed of delivery. Here's how we handle each:

Consent: [TrustedForm / Jornaya] certificate on every lead, retained 5+ years, produced on audit request.

Qualification: [your qualification protocol; mention if it's call-verified vs. form-only].

Speed: [your typical delivery latency from form submission to buyer CRM].

If any of those would be a deal-breaker if not met, I'd rather know now than have you find out three weeks in.

"What's your volume — can you scale with us?"

Honest answer: I'm currently producing [_____ leads / week] and have head-room to roughly double in the near term. If your needs are above that, I'm probably not your only-vendor — but I can be a steady share of your supply. Want to start with a sample batch and see how the quality compares?

Script 5 — The negotiation conversation

Once a buyer has accepted a sample batch and the leads convert acceptably, the conversation moves to terms.

Pricing

Based on the sample, our current pricing for [shared / exclusive] leads in this sub-vertical is [$X]. Volume tiers start at [$Y per lead] above [N leads/month].

Open to discussing a starter rate for the first [30 / 60 / 90] days while we build the track record together — would [$Z] for the first [period] work, with a step-up to [$X] after?

Payment terms

For new relationships, our standard is [50% prepayment / weekly invoicing / net 15] until we've established a track record together. We can move to standard terms after [30-90] days.

If that's a deal-breaker, can we do prepayment for the first [N] leads as a demonstration of good faith, then move to weekly invoicing after?

Exclusivity

Our exclusive pricing for [sub-vertical] is [$X]. At your volume of [N leads/month], we can guarantee exclusivity in [geography] if we commit at that volume. Want to scope what exclusivity would mean for your intake math?

The walking-away conversation

Some buyers will not be workable. If you're hitting hard pushback on TCPA reps, audit rights, indemnification, or quality definitions, walk. These are the protections that make the business sustainable; agreeing to weaken them to win a deal is a way to lose the business.

Phrasing: "I appreciate the time, but the terms you're describing aren't workable for me — I need [specific clause] to stay as-is or I can't do this profitably. If something changes on your end, please reach out. Otherwise good luck with your supply search."

Standing disclosures

These scripts are research-based starting points, not finished sales playbooks. Use them as raw material; adapt to your voice, your vertical, and your specific situation. The course author is a curriculum builder, not a multi-million-dollar lead-gen operator; specific tactical claims in trade publications and operator podcasts are the underlying sources for the patterns above.
