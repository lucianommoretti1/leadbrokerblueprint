Standard Operating Procedures — Intake and Lead Delivery

What's in this document

Two sample Standard Operating Procedures (SOPs) that translate Module 6's operational guidance into step-by-step playbooks: one for inbound and outbound intake, one for lead delivery and dispute handling.

These are templates. Adapt the steps to your vertical, your distribution platform, your CRM, and your specific buyer requirements. The structure (purpose, scope, roles, steps, evidence, escalation) is the part to preserve.

SOP 1 — Lead Intake (phone-based)

Purpose

Standardize phone-based intake on inbound calls (from click-to-call or pay-per-call) and outbound qualification of form-submitted leads. Goal: consistent qualified-lead output, defensible compliance posture, and an evidentiary record for every consumer interaction.

Scope

Applies to all phone-based intake interactions for lead inventory delivered to buyers under any active lead purchase agreement. Excludes pure form leads that are not phone-qualified before delivery.

Roles

  • Intake agent — conducts the call, performs qualification, records outcomes in CRM.
  • Intake supervisor — reviews quality samples weekly; coaches; handles escalations.
  • Compliance lead — owns scripts, disclosure language, recording-disclosure policy.

Equipment

  • Call platform (Ringba / CallRail / equivalent) with recording enabled and state-by-state recording disclosure configured.
  • CRM (vertical-appropriate) with intake-call template open.
  • Approved intake script (current version date) and qualification questions specific to the lead's buyer assignment.

Procedure

Step 1 — Opening (within 5 seconds of connect)

State your name and the operation name. Confirm you're speaking to the right person. State recording disclosure if required by the consumer's state. Confirm consent to continue with the call.

Script anchor (varies by vertical): "Hi [Consumer], this is [Agent] with [Operation]. I'm following up on the [service] request you submitted at [time]. This call is being recorded for quality assurance. Have a few minutes to talk?"

Step 2 — Qualification questions (in order)

Ask qualification questions in the order specified for the lead's vertical/buyer. Do not improvise the order; consistent ordering supports comparability and dispute defense. Record each answer in the CRM template.

If the consumer disqualifies on any hard-stop question, proceed to Step 4 (disqualified close).

Step 3 — Qualified consumer: next-step setup

Confirm contact information (name, phone, email, address). Explain what happens next: who will call the consumer, in what time window, and what to expect on that call. If transferring live, transfer within Ringba/Phonexa to the buyer's intake line.

Step 4 — Disqualified consumer: close

Polite close per the script's disqualified-close section. Do not transfer to a different vertical or attempt to re-qualify against different criteria. Note the disqualification reason in the CRM.

Step 5 — Revocation handling

If at any point the consumer asks to stop being contacted, to be removed from any list, or otherwise revokes consent: acknowledge immediately, end the marketing portion of the call, and document the revocation in the CRM with timestamp. The revocation must propagate to all downstream parties within 10 business days (FCC Revocation Rule, effective April 11, 2025).

Step 6 — Wrap and documentation

End the call politely. Within 5 minutes of the call ending, complete all CRM fields: qualification answers, contact information, outcome (qualified / disqualified / revoked), next step (delivered to buyer / scheduled / disqualified), and any free-text notes.

Evidence and audit

  • Call recording retained in the call-tracking platform (minimum 5 years; longer for Medicare per CMS).
  • CRM entry with timestamp, agent ID, and outcome.
  • TrustedForm / Jornaya certificate URL for the underlying form submission, linked to the CRM record.

Quality monitoring

  • Intake supervisor reviews 3-5 calls per agent per week using a scoring rubric.
  • Weekly team review of common disqualification reasons and edge cases.
  • Monthly review of agent-level metrics: qualified-lead rate, average call duration, dispute rate on delivered leads.

Escalation

  • Consumer disputes recording → end call, escalate to supervisor immediately.
  • Consumer alleges they never opted in → end call, document, escalate to compliance lead within 1 business day.
  • Threats of legal action → end call, document, escalate to founder/counsel within 4 business hours.

Revision history

Document version, date, and revision notes maintained by the compliance lead. Re-review quarterly alongside regulatory updates.

SOP 2 — Lead Delivery and Dispute Handling

Purpose

Standardize how leads are routed to buyers, how delivery is confirmed, how disputes and returns are evaluated, and how revocations propagate across the chain. Goal: predictable delivery, auditable handling, and clean dispute resolution.

Scope

Applies to all leads produced for sale to buyers under any active lead purchase agreement. Covers form leads, qualified phone leads, and live-transfer calls.

Roles

  • Operations agent — monitors lead distribution platform queue; handles routing exceptions; processes returns.
  • Account manager — buyer-facing point of contact for dispute discussions, weekly reporting, contract questions.
  • Compliance lead — owns disclosure changes, TrustedForm configuration, revocation propagation policy.

Procedure

Step 1 — Lead capture

Consumer submits form (or initiates click-to-call). Landing page captures TrustedForm or Jornaya certificate. Lead is posted to LeadConduit (or equivalent) via HTTP POST or API. Lead record in CRM is created within 5 seconds of capture.

Step 2 — Validation

LeadConduit (or equivalent) runs configured filters:

  • Phone number validation.
  • Address validation.
  • DNC scrub (within the regulators' timing window, currently no more than 31 days).
  • Duplicate-detection against the prior [window] of submissions.
  • Vertical-specific qualification (e.g., zip code in service area; minimum age; injury within window).

Leads failing validation are flagged and not routed to buyers.

Step 3 — Routing

LeadConduit applies the routing rules per active lead purchase agreement: matches lead attributes to buyer specifications; respects exclusivity provisions; for shared inventory, posts to up to N specified buyers within the geography. Confirmation of successful post is captured per buyer.

Step 4 — Delivery confirmation

Each delivery generates a delivery record in LeadConduit with: lead ID, buyer ID, timestamp, HTTP response code, and TrustedForm certificate URL. The delivery record is retained for at least five years.

Step 5 — Dispute / return intake

Buyer submits dispute via [email / portal / API]. Operations agent acknowledges receipt within 1 business day. Operations agent applies the Lead-Quality Dispute Decision Tree (Module 4 deliverable):

  1. Within return window? (per LPA Section 5.2)
  2. Contractually enumerated return reason? (per LPA Exhibit C)
  3. Evidence provided?
  4. Evaluate evidence against own records.
  5. Decision: accept / reject / split.
  6. Notify buyer with decision and reasoning.

Step 6 — Revocation propagation

If a buyer or upstream party reports a consumer revocation:

  1. Operations agent records the revocation in LeadConduit (or equivalent) within 1 business day of receipt.
  2. LeadConduit propagates the revocation to all parties that received the underlying lead — every shared buyer, every generator, every aggregator in the chain.
  3. Total propagation must complete within 10 business days per the April 11, 2025 FCC Revocation Rule.
  4. Audit log retained showing each propagation step and timestamp.

Step 7 — Weekly reporting to buyers

Account manager sends each active buyer a weekly summary: leads delivered, returns submitted and processed, dispute resolutions, and any open items. Reporting cadence and format match the buyer's preference; consistency matters more than feature-richness.

Evidence and audit

  • LeadConduit (or equivalent) delivery records retained 5+ years.
  • TrustedForm / Jornaya certificate URLs retained 5+ years.
  • Dispute case files with all communications retained 5+ years.
  • Revocation propagation audit log retained 5+ years.

Quality monitoring

  • Daily: queue health (any leads stuck in validation, any delivery failures).
  • Weekly: per-buyer dispute rate, per-source defect rate, validation-failure rate.
  • Monthly: end-to-end audit of 10 random leads — capture → validation → delivery → outcome — to verify the audit trail is intact.
  • Quarterly: compliance-checklist review (Module 3 deliverable) covering this SOP and adjacent ones.

Escalation

  • Delivery system outage → on-call ops contact within 15 minutes; downstream buyers notified within 1 hour.
  • Buyer alleges systemic quality issue → account manager + ops + compliance review within 1 business day.
  • Regulator inquiry or class-action notice → counsel notified within 4 business hours; preserve all relevant records immediately.

Revision history

Document version, date, and revision notes maintained by the compliance lead. Re-review quarterly alongside regulatory updates.

Standing disclosures

These are template SOPs, not operating procedures finished for any specific operation. Adapt them to your vertical, your distribution platform, your CRM, and your specific buyer requirements. Counsel familiar with your operation should review the final versions. The course author is a curriculum builder, not a multi-million-dollar lead-gen operator; the templates draw on operator-published patterns and trade-publication content.
